Five possible outcomes

Execute
Every required gate passes within delegated authority.
Ask
Collect specific missing evidence before deciding again.
Defer
Wait for fresh evidence or a healthy dependency.
Escalate
An authorized operator must resolve a scope, confidence, or risk limit.
Refuse
A hard policy prohibition prevents the action.

What the numbers mean

Confidence is a transparent evidence-quality heuristic, not a calibrated probability of success. It uses the weakest source reliability and penalties for missing or stale evidence.

Risk is an additive policy score. Its components are visible in each result. Financial exposure, missing evidence, privileged access, and irreversible effects increase it.

Request text never controls either number. The policy is versioned, and execution always rechecks it.

What is real in this demo

The server, SQLite storage, policy evaluation, audit hashes, budget tracking, and execution gates are working code. Actions update persistent sandbox records.

Evidence and proposals are synthetic fixtures. No external payment, deployment, or directory service is connected. An optional AgentRouter connection lets Claude propose actions. Its output is validated and checked by the policy engine before a separate execution step.

A hash chain detects edits relative to its stored chain. Without an external signed anchor, it cannot prove an administrator has not rewritten the entire ledger.

The two-year thesis

Agent autonomy will be purchased in increments, not granted as a switch. The valuable layer will be the boundary between a plausible intention and a permitted effect.

That boundary must combine evidence provenance, scoped delegation, cumulative exposure, reversibility, and the consequences of being wrong. A confident model output is only a proposal. It cannot sign its own permission slip.

As agents operate across companies, decision records will become portable receipts: what was known, which policy allowed an action, who held authority, and what changed between approval and execution. Execution-time checks matter as much as the initial decision.

The winning systems will make refusal and escalation usable. They will collect missing evidence, route work to the right operator, measure outcomes, and calibrate uncertainty against real failures. Human review will concentrate on novel risks while repeatable, bounded work earns autonomy through evidence.
