# Two-year thesis

Agent autonomy will be purchased in increments, not granted as a switch. The valuable layer will be the boundary between a plausible intention and a permitted effect.

That boundary must combine evidence provenance, scoped delegation, cumulative exposure, reversibility, and the consequences of being wrong. A confident model output is only a proposal. It cannot sign its own permission slip.

As agents operate across companies, decision records will become portable receipts: what was known, which policy allowed an action, who held authority, and what changed between approval and execution. Execution-time checks matter as much as the initial decision.

The winning systems will make refusal and escalation usable. They will collect missing evidence, route work to the right operator, measure outcomes, and calibrate uncertainty against real failures. Human review will concentrate on novel risks while repeatable, bounded work earns autonomy through evidence.
